About

Infinity Space Club is the student space-technology club at UPES. We build things that fly, listen to things in orbit, and once a year hand the campus a real problem to solve.

What we do

  • High-altitude balloons. Payloads that climb past 30 km and come back down with data.
  • CanSats and model rockets. A full design, build and launch cycle every semester.
  • Our ground station. A rooftop antenna that pulls down images from weather satellites as they pass over Dehradun.
  • Sky nights. Telescopes and star charts on clear nights in the hills above campus.

About Apogee

Apogee is the point in an orbit farthest from Earth, and the name of our flagship hackathon.

Each year we pick one real problem, open it to students from any college and any branch, and give teams 36 hours, mentors from industry and research labs, and all the chai they can drink.

This year’s problem comes from the mountains just north of us.

Our mission

Make space technology hands-on for every student at UPES, and point it at problems close to home.

  • Build, don’t just watch

    Every member works on real hardware or real satellite data in their first semester with us.

  • Learn in the open

    Designs, code and what went wrong all go into a shared archive, so each batch starts further ahead.

  • Start close to home

    We look for problems in the Himalaya and the Doon valley before we look anywhere else.

Problem statement

Build an early-warning system for Himalayan glacial lakes.

As Himalayan glaciers retreat, meltwater collects in lakes held back by walls of loose rock and ice called moraines. When one of those walls gives way, a glacial lake outburst flood can reach villages, roads and hydropower projects downstream within hours.

Many of these lakes sit above 4,000 m, far from roads and power lines, so ground sensors are hard to install and harder to maintain. For months each monsoon, clouds hide them from optical satellites.

Your challenge: design a system that spots a dangerous change in a glacial lake early enough for people downstream to act. Pick one of two tracks.

Track A

Ground segment

Software and open satellite data

Use open satellite data to map glacial lakes in Uttarakhand, measure how each one has changed, and flag the ones that need attention. Present it in a tool a district disaster-management officer could use on an ordinary laptop.

You submit

  • A working prototype
  • Source code in a public repository
  • A 5-minute demo

Track B

Space segment

CubeSat mission design

Design a 3U CubeSat mission that closes a gap in today’s coverage: blindness in cloudy months, long waits between passes, or slow delivery of data to the ground. Justify your payload, orbit, power budget and link budget.

You submit

  • A mission design document, 10 pages at most
  • A 5-minute pitch

Open data you can use

  • Sentinel-1 radar imagery Sees through cloud, day or night. Copernicus Data Space (opens in a new tab)
  • Sentinel-2 optical imagery 10 m colour images every few days. Copernicus Data Space (opens in a new tab)
  • Landsat 8 and 9 Plus the older Landsat archive for long-term change. USGS EarthExplorer (opens in a new tab)
  • Bhuvan ISRO’s geoportal for Indian maps and datasets. bhuvan.nrsc.gov.in (opens in a new tab)

How we judge

  • Usefulness to people downstream30%
  • Technical soundness30%
  • Feasibility with today’s data and budgets20%
  • Clarity of the demo or pitch20%

Finalists present to a panel of mentors and guest judges on 22 November.

Ground rules

  • Teams of 2 to 4 students. Mix branches, years and colleges if you like.
  • Build during the 36 hours. Open-source libraries, public datasets and pre-trained models are allowed.
  • Bring your own laptops. We provide power, Wi-Fi, meals and a quiet room for naps.
